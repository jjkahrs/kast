SDFGI

SDFGI (signed distance field global illumination) gives a whole open world diffuse bounce light, sky occlusion and rough reflections with no bake. Four nested grids (cascades) of a signed distance field and a probe grid follow the camera; the GPU builds them from the scene's Static geometry and its streamed terrain, relights them every frame with the scene's lights and sky, and meshes take their ambient light from the probes. Turn the sun and the bounce light under every overhang follows; stream in a new terrain tile and it has GI a few frames later (engine/sdfgi.h, engine/shaders/cs_sdfgi_*.sc, engine/shaders/fs_mesh.sc). The Scene view, the Game view and the built game render it alike; each viewport's cascades follow its own camera.

It is a per-scene setting, off by default: a scene with SDFGI off renders pixel for pixel as before.

Quick start

  1. Set the buildings, walls, rocks and other fixed geometry to GI Mode Static (their Mesh Renderer or Model Renderer; Lightmaps and GI). Static is what enters the distance field: what bounces and blocks SDFGI's light. Terrain tiles always do.
  2. Window > Lighting > SDFGI > Enabled. That is all: the cascades build in the next frame.
  3. Tune Cell Size to the scene's scale (0.5 m, the default, suits buildings; a smaller cell resolves thinner walls over a smaller area) and Energy to taste. Everything applies at once.

tests/project/sdfgi_outdoor.scene is the example: the hills map's terrain around a plinth with a red and a green wall, a white back wall and an overhang, a Dynamic ball, the sun and the procedural sky; in Play its camera flies a path (scripts/sdfgi_camera_path.lua).

A field out of range (or of the wrong type, or unknown) doesn't fail the scene: it loads with SDFGI off and one warning naming it, e.g. lighting.sdfgi: "cellSize" must be a number between 0.125 and 4; SDFGI left off. The Lighting panel's SDFGI section edits them all (undoable, applied at once).

How it works

Cascades

Each cascade is a grid of 64 x 64 x 64 cells (64 x 32 x 64 or 64 x 16 x 64 with yScale) centered on the camera, its cell size twice the last's: with the defaults 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 m, so the cascades are 32, 64, 128 and 256 m wide. A cascade's origin snaps to its probe spacing (4 cells), so it moves in whole probe slabs. Its textures are toroidal: a cell at global position g lives at texel g mod 64, so when the camera moves the cells still in the cascade keep their texels and only the slabs that came into it are rebuilt (sdfgiScrollRegions: up to three disjoint slabs, one per axis moved). A cascade is only rebuilt whole when the camera jumps past its extent (a teleport), which is logged (SDFGI: cascade 0 rebuilt in full (moved past its extent)), and when its settings change.

The distance field

Per cell (cs_sdfgi_build.sc): the signed distance, clamped to 4 cells, to the nearest surface, and that surface's albedo (its material's base color) and emission:

  • Static primitives: cubes and planes as oriented boxes (a plane as a 10 cm sliver), spheres as ellipsoids, from their world transform (exact for boxes).
  • Static models: each submesh as the box of its world bounds.
  • Terrain tiles: a heightfield. Each cascade keeps a column per cell (x, z) holding the ground's height at its center and its slope; a cell's distance is its height above the ground times the cosine of the slope (exact for a plane; checked against brute force in sdfgi_self_check).

Every frame the renderer diffs the Static shapes and the streamed tiles against the last frame's: a shape added, removed, moved or recolored in the editor, or a tile streamed in or out, rebuilds the cells within reach of it in every cascade, that frame.

Lighting and probes

  1. Light (cs_sdfgi_light.sc, every cell of every cascade, every frame): a cell on a surface is lit where the surface crosses it, its normal the distance field's gradient: the sun and up to 16 local lights (point, spot, area, with the falloffs fs_mesh.sc uses), each shadowed by marching the distance field toward it, plus bounceFeedback times its cascade's probes' light there (so light bounces once more every frame), times its albedo, plus its emission.
  2. Probes (cs_sdfgi_probe.sc): a probe every 4 cells (16 x 16 x 16 per cascade, 16 384 in all) casts the preset's rays, a spherical Fibonacci set turned anew each frame, through the distance field (a ray leaving its cascade goes on in the next, coarser one). A hit takes the lit cell's light; a ray that leaves the last cascade sees the sky (its harmonics, or the flat ambient without a sky). The rays' first-order spherical harmonics blend into the probe's (temporal accumulation: the preset's hysteresis of last frame's kept). A probe whose cell is inside geometry is marked invalid and skipped by the shading.

Shading

A mesh that takes SDFGI (below) samples the finest cascade holding the shaded point: the 8 probes around it after pushing it a quarter of a probe spacing off the surface along its normal, each weighted by its trilinear weight times ((n . toward the probe + 1) / 2)^2 + 0.05 (probes behind the surface count little), invalid ones left out (sdfgiProbeWeights). Over a cascade's outer two probe spacings it fades into the next cascade, and past the last into the light the mesh gets without SDFGI, so nothing pops at the edges.

diffuse  = SDFGI(p, n) = energy * (a0 + 2/3 a1 . n)       // irradiance / pi from the probes' harmonics
specular = rough surfaces (roughness 0.4 .. 0.6 blending in): energy * (a0 + a1 . r) * split-sum BRDF
           smooth ones: the environment's (sky or flat ambient) as before

Rough reflections come from SDFGI above a roughness of 0.5 (blended from 0.4 to 0.6): the probes' radiance along the reflection replaces the sky's prefiltered cube, so a rough wall under an overhang no longer reflects the open sky. A mesh inside a ReflectionProbe's box keeps the probe's specular (a baked probe sees its room better than the coarse SDFGI probes); smooth surfaces keep the sky's.

Precedence

SDFGI fills its slot in the one selection point: Lightmap > VoxelGI > SDFGI > probe grid > environment. A Static mesh with a valid lightmap keeps its lightmap (and its specular); a mesh whose bounds center is in an enabled, baked VoxelGI volume keeps the volume's light; every other mesh (Off, Dynamic, Static without a lightmap, terrain without one) takes SDFGI. Lightmapped and VoxelGI geometry still enters the distance field when Static, so it bounces and blocks SDFGI's light for the meshes around it. Tested in lightmap_room and voxelgi_room with SDFGI on: the lightmapped floor and walls and every mesh in the VoxelGI room render exactly as without SDFGI, the Dynamic ball takes SDFGI instead of the probe grid.

Meshes with a .kshader material get the light below SDFGI (the probe grid or the environment): SDFGI lives in a variant of the built-in mesh shader (fs_mesh.sc compiled with KAST_SDFGI) that only the draws taking SDFGI use, so every other draw runs exactly the code it ran before SDFGI existed (and scenes without SDFGI render pixel-identical).

Dynamic meshes (and anything not Static: skinned models, particles) receive SDFGI but never enter the distance field: they neither bounce nor block its light. SDFGI's shadows are the distance field's, not the shadow maps', so a Dynamic mesh doesn't darken it either (tested: the scene without the Dynamic ball is pixel-identical away from the ball and its direct shadow).

Terrain streaming

A tile that streams in enters the distance field the frame it appears (its columns in every cascade, the cells above them rebuilt and relit); a tile that streams out leaves the same way. The probes around it see it from the next frame on and blend it into their light at the preset's hysteresis (restarting them instead converged slower, noisier), so 10 frames after a tile streamed in its GI is within a mean difference of 1 / 255 of where it settles (sg_stream_lit_in_time: measured 0.58 / 255, and 5 / 255 away from the same view without SDFGI). With --log-sdfgi the editor and the player log SDFGI: terrain tile 1_0 streamed in / streamed out.

Along the path the 99th percentile frame took 1.00 / 1.17 / 1.19 ms (Low / Medium / High), the SDFGI view 0.20 / 0.63 / 0.60 ms; repeated runs vary by up to about 50% with the GPU's clocks. The budget is 4 ms at Medium, frames where the cascades scroll included: player_sdfgi_gpu_time checks the 99th percentile frame along the path (the slowest frame is logged too, but a lone hiccup of the OS or another program on the GPU, seen now and then at 3 to 10 ms with the camera still, doesn't fail it). Scrolling costs little: a probe slab of one cascade is 4 x 64 x 64 cells. The light and probe passes run every frame over all four cascades whatever the camera does; the shading cost grows with the pixels that take SDFGI. The stats overlay (F3 in the Game view and the player) adds SDFGI <ms> ms, the SDFGI view's GPU time; --log-sdfgi logs each frame's rebuilt cells per cascade. GPU memory per viewport at the defaults: about 21 MB (four 643 cascades of albedo, distance + emission and light, two probe textures, the terrain columns).

Leaks and limitations

  • Thin walls: a wall thinner than a cell (0.5 m in the first cascade, 4 m in the last) has no cell inside it, so light can leak through, more so far from the camera where the cells are coarse. Mitigations: probes inside geometry are skipped, the shaded point is pushed off the surface along its normal, and probes behind the surface weigh little; there is no per-probe visibility (depth moments), so a probe on the far side of a thin wall can still bleed through. Make walls at least a cell thick, or lower cellSize, where interiors matter. Indoors, a VoxelGI volume or lightmaps win over SDFGI and hold walls better.
  • Models enter the field as their submeshes' bounding boxes (solid): a hollow or concave model blocks light inside its bounds. Albedo is the material's base color factor; textures are ignored.
  • Low frequency: the probes are 2 m apart at the defaults (16 m in the last cascade) and store first-order harmonics, so contact shadows and small details still come from the shadow maps and the direct light.
  • The sky is seen through its spherical harmonics (no sun disk in the bounce); emissive surfaces light through the probes only (no direct emissive term).
  • At most 16 local lights (the nearest the cascades' center) light the field. D3D11 compute and 3D images are required: without them SDFGI is off with one warning (SDFGI: this GPU lacks compute or the 3D image formats it needs; SDFGI is off) and meshes get the light below it.
  • Nothing is baked or saved: there are no SDFGI files, in the project or in game.pak.

Lua

kast.sdfgi.get() returns the settings as a table; kast.sdfgi.set{...} changes enabled and energy at run time (all-or-nothing, checked as on load; the other fields rebuild the cascades and belong to the scene, so setting one is an error).

kast.sdfgi.set({energy = 0.5})      -- dusk: half the bounced light
kast.sdfgi.set({enabled = false})   -- off: the probe grid or the sky instead
print(kast.sdfgi.get().quality)     -- "medium"

Moving the sun from Lua (kast.set on its Transform) changes the bounce light live: the light pass reads the scene's lights every frame (scripts/sdfgi_sun.lua).
