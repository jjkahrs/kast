SSAO

Screen-space ambient occlusion darkens the ambient light in creases, corners and where objects meet the ground, computed every frame from the depth buffer and the view-space normals (ground-truth AO, GTAO: Jimenez et al. 2016, with XeGTAO's falloff). It only takes away indirect diffuse light (flat ambient, IBL / reflection probes, lightmaps, VoxelGI, SDFGI): direct light, specular and emission are never darkened, so a surface lit only by a lamp is unchanged (engine/post_process.h, engine/shaders/post/fs_ssao_*.sc). The Scene view (with its toolbar's Post box), the Game view and the built game apply it alike.

It is a per-scene setting ("postProcessing" > "ssao"), off by default: a scene without it renders exactly as before. Edit it in Window > Post Processing > SSAO (live, undoable, marks the scene dirty) or from Lua.

Settings

FieldDefaultRangeMeaning
enabledfalseSSAO on
radius0.50.05 to 5meters around a point within which geometry occludes it
intensity10 to 4scales the occlusion (0: no change, byte for byte)
power1.50.5 to 4falloff: higher darkens the creases more and open surfaces less
sliceCount2integer 1 to 4horizon search directions per pixel (quality and cost)
"postProcessing": {
  "ssao": { "enabled": true, "radius": 0.5, "intensity": 1, "power": 1.5, "sliceCount": 2 }
}

Every field is optional (missing ones take the default). An unknown field, a wrong type or a value out of range fails the scene's load with an error naming it, e.g. postProcessing.ssao: "radius" must be a number between 0.05 and 5, and the open scene is left untouched, as for the other post effects.

How it works

  1. G-data: while SSAO or SSR is on, opaque and alpha-masked meshes (every fs_mesh.sc variant, .kshaders and shader graphs) also write their view-space normal and roughness (RGB10A2, octahedral) and their indirect diffuse light (RG11B10F, already times the material's AO and the distance fog's remaining fraction) next to the scene color. Blended meshes, particles and the sky don't (cleared to 0: no surface). About 24 MiB at 1920 x 1080, allocated only while needed.
  2. GTAO, half resolution: for sliceCount directions around the view vector (rotated and jittered by a 4 x 4 Bayer pattern), the highest horizon either side within radius (6 steps a side, samples fading to the open horizon over the radius's outer 61.5%, at most 64 half-res pixels), integrated against the normal's cosine. Output: visibility v 0..1 (1 open).
  3. Blur: a bilateral 4 x 4 box at half resolution (taps weighed by depth within 5% + 1 cm and normal agreement), which averages out the 4 x 4 dither. No temporal history: every frame stands alone.
  4. Composite, full resolution (depth-aware 2 x 2 upsample), subtracted from the scene color: 
    occlusion = saturate(intensity * (1 - v^power))
color     = color - indirectDiffuse * occlusion
    (kast::aoComposite is the CPU mirror checked by engine_self_check.)

Cost

Debug build, RTX 4070 Ti SUPER, built player at 1920 x 1080 (--gpu-stats, mean of 3 runs of 590 frames), on tests/project/ssao.scene: 2.93 ms GPU frame with SSAO vs 2.51 ms off, about +0.4 ms for the G-data and the three passes together. Memory: the G-data (24 MiB) plus two half-res RG16F targets (4 MiB).

Lua

kast.ssao.get() returns the settings as a table; kast.ssao.set{...} changes any of its fields at run time. It is all-or-nothing and checked as on load: a bad field changes nothing and raises an error such as kast.ssao.set: postProcessing.ssao: "sliceCount" must be an integer between 1 and 4.

kast.ssao.set({enabled = true, radius = 1, intensity = 2})
print(kast.ssao.get().sliceCount)   -- 2

See tests/project/ssao.scene (a cube, a sphere and boxes on the ground, in a corner and stacked) and scripts/ssao_set.lua.
