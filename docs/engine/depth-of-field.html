Depth of field

Depth of field blurs what is nearer or farther than the focus distance, like a real camera lens: a physical thin-lens circle of confusion, disc-shaped bokeh on bright highlights, and a near field that blurs softly over in-focus edges (engine/post_process.h, engine/shaders/post/fs_dof_*.sc, dof.sh). Focus is manual: set it from the scene or from Lua (a cutscene's focus pull). The Scene view follows its toolbar's Post box like every other effect; the Game view and the built game always apply it.

It is a per-scene setting ("postProcessing" > "depthOfField"), not per camera, off by default: a scene without it renders exactly as before. Edit it in Window > Post Processing > Depth of Field (live, undoable, marks the scene dirty) or from Lua.

Settings

FieldDefaultRangeMeaning
enabledfalsedepth of field on
focusDistance100.1 to 10000meters from the camera that are sharp
fStop2.80.7 to 32aperture N: smaller is a shallower focus band and bigger blur
focalLength508 to 400millimeters; longer is more blur. It sets only the blur: the camera's field of view is unchanged
maxCoc161 to 64pixels: the largest blur radius (at the image's height in pixels, any resolution)
"postProcessing": {
  "depthOfField": { "enabled": true, "focusDistance": 6, "fStop": 1.4, "focalLength": 85, "maxCoc": 24 }
}

Every field is optional (missing ones take the default). An unknown field, a wrong type or a value out of range fails the scene's load with an error naming it, e.g. postProcessing.depthOfField: "fStop" must be a number between 0.7 and 32, and the open scene is left untouched.

Circle of confusion

A 35 mm full-frame sensor (36 x 24 mm) is assumed; its 24 mm height is matched to the image's height, so the blur looks the same at any aspect ratio. With focal length f and focus distance S in millimeters, f-stop N, image height H pixels and view depth z, the signed CoC radius in pixels is:

scale = f^2 / (N * (S - f)) / 2 * H / 24 mm
coc   = clamp(scale * (z - S) / z, -maxCoc, maxCoc)      (< 0 near field, > 0 far field)

It is 0 at the focus distance, grows with defocus, and doubles when the f-stop halves (kast::circleOfConfusion and kast::dofCocScale are the CPU mirrors checked by engine_self_check).

How it works

  1. Setup, half resolution (RGBA16F): color and signed CoC of each 2 x 2 block; the nearest's CoC if any is in the near field (so the near blur reaches past its silhouette), else their mean.
  2. Gather, half resolution, two layers over a 64-tap Vogel disc. Far: taps over the pixel's own CoC, kept where their CoC reaches it, equal weights, so bright highlights become flat discs and a sharp foreground never smears into the blurred background. Near: taps over maxCoc, weighed by 1 / coc^2, with a coverage that falls off smoothly past an object's edge: near blur bleeds over in-focus pixels without a hard halo.
  3. Composite, full resolution, into its own RGBA16F target that bloom, tonemap and FXAA then read: the sharp color blends to the far layer as its CoC grows past half a pixel, then the near layer goes over by its coverage.

Order: SSAO / SSR / volumetric fog composites, blended meshes and particles, depth of field, then bloom and the tonemap (so blurred highlights still bloom, and blended draws blur with the scene). Limit: heavily blurred near edges show a faint dotted grain; CoC tile dilation and scatter are the upgrade path.

Cost

Debug build, RTX 4070 Ti SUPER, built player at 1920 x 1080 (--gpu-stats, mean of 3 runs of 590 frames), on tests/project/dof.scene: 3.22 ms GPU frame with depth of field vs 2.61 ms off, about +0.6 ms. Memory about 28 MiB at 1920 x 1080 (three half-res and one full-res RGBA16F target), made on first use and freed when off.

Lua

kast.dof.get() returns the settings as a table; kast.dof.set{...} changes any of its fields at run time, all-or-nothing and checked as on load: kast.dof.set: postProcessing.depthOfField: "fStop" must be a number between 0.7 and 32.

-- cutscene focus pull: from the near character to the far one
kast.dof.set({enabled = true, focusDistance = 14, fStop = 2})

See tests/project/dof.scene (rows of objects at three depths, emissive lights far away) and scripts/dof_set.lua.
