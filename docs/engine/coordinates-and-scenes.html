Coordinates and scenes

  • Right-handed, Y up, 1 unit = 1 meter. Cameras and directional lights look down their local -Z.
  • Rotations are stored as standard (Hamilton, glTF-style) quaternions [x, y, z, w]. The Inspector shows Euler degrees (X pitch, Y yaw, Z roll), applied Z, then X, then Y.
  • Matrices follow bgfx/bx: row vectors, translation in elements 12–14, world = local * parentWorld.
  • Built-in primitives fit the unit cube centered on the origin: cube (1 m), plane (1x1 m on XZ, facing +Y), sphere (0.5 m radius).
  • Lighting (forward; shading model under Materials): ambient + directional + point, spot and area lights.
    • Ambient: per scene, "lighting" in the scene file, edited in Window > Lighting. Default white at 0.2. With the sky off it is a flat color; with the sky on, the sky's image-based lighting, scaled and tinted by it; inside a baked ReflectionProbe's box, the probe's (Image-based lighting).
    • Sky: per scene, "sky" in the scene file, edited in the same panel: the background, and the ambient light while it is on (Skybox).
    • Directional: the first DirectionalLight in hierarchy order; others are ignored.
    • Point: every PointLight, from its entity's world position, falling off smoothly to exactly 0 at range (saturate(1 - (d/r)^4)^2 / (d^2 + 1)). Selecting one in the Scene view shows its range.
    • Spot: every SpotLight, from its entity's world position along its world -Z (scale ignored): the point light falloff times a smooth cone edge, 1 inside innerAngle falling to 0 at outerAngle (half-angles in degrees, smoothstep of the cosine). Nothing outside the outer cone or beyond range. Selecting one shows its outer cone out to its range and the ring of its inner angle.
    • Area: every AreaLight, a width x height m rectangle on its entity's XY plane, centered on it, emitting toward its world -Z (scale ignored); with twoSided it emits both ways, else nothing reaches behind it. Shaded with Linearly Transformed Cosines (Heitz et al. 2016): the diffuse is the rect's exact form factor, the GGX specular comes from a 16x16 table fit to shade()'s BRDF (tools/ltc_fit), both clipped to the surface's horizon, so glossy surfaces reflect it as a rectangle. color * intensity is its radiance in the units of the other lights: a surface whose whole view it fills gets what a point light gives at 0 m (so a small, far panel needs a higher intensity than a point light). It reaches range meters from its center, fading out smoothly (saturate(1 - (d/r)^4)^2; no inverse square, the form factor falls off itself). Selecting one shows the rect, an arrow the way it shines and a circle of its range.
    • At most 8 local lights per mesh: point, spot and area lights share 8 slots per draw, the lights nearest the mesh's bounds center that reach its bounds (point and area lights by their range sphere, spot lights also by their outer cone); the rest don't light that mesh.
    • Shadows (realtime shadow maps, in the Scene view, the Game view and the built game; no ambient occlusion, baked or contact shadows):
      • Directional: cascaded shadow maps when the DirectionalLight's castShadows is on (default). The view from the camera's near plane to maxDistance (default 100 m, or the camera's far plane if nearer) splits into cascadeCount (1–4, default 4) cascades (a 0.75 mix of logarithmic and uniform splits), each a 2048x2048 tile in the top half (2x2 tiles) of one 4096x8192 16-bit depth atlas. Each cascade is fit to a bounding sphere of its slice and moves in whole texels, so shadow edges don't shimmer as the camera moves or turns. Beyond maxDistance nothing is shadowed (a hard cut, no fade). Hardware depth comparison with 3x3 PCF.
      • Point: a 1024x1024-per-face 32-bit cube shadow map for each PointLight with castShadows on (default off), shadowing in every direction from 0.05 m to its range. At most 4 per frame: the castShadows lights nearest the camera; the rest still light, unshadowed. 3x3 PCF across the face.
      • Spot and area: a 2048x2048 perspective tile in the bottom half (2x2 tiles) of the same atlas for each SpotLight or AreaLight with castShadows on (default off), from the light (an area light's center) along its direction out to its range: a spot's tile is its outer cone wide, an area light's 120 degrees. At most 4 per frame (a pool apart from the point lights' 4 cubes): the castShadows spot and area lights nearest the camera. 3x3 PCF for spots; area lights get a wider 5x5 PCF 1.5 texels apart. Approximation: an area light shadows as if all its light came from its center, so its shadows don't widen with the rect's size, and a two-sided light's back side is unshadowed.
      • Acne vs. peter-panning: DirectionalLight.bias (meters along the light, default 0.05) and normalBias (shadow-map texels along the surface normal, default 1.5); PointLight, SpotLight and AreaLight bias (meters toward the light, default 0.05; plus a fixed one-texel normal offset). Raise them for acne (striped self-shadowing), lower them if shadows detach from their casters.
      • Per mesh: MeshRenderer / ModelRenderer castShadows and receiveShadows (both default on). Alpha-mask materials cast a cutout shadow (alpha-tested in the depth pass); alpha-blend materials never cast but do receive. Skinned models cast their current pose.
      • Resolutions are engine constants (engine/shadow.h); shadow maps are created on first use, per renderer (the editor's Scene and Game views have one each). Each render path reserves Renderer::kShadowViews (32: 4 cascades, 24 cube faces, 4 spot / area tiles) bgfx views before its scene view: editor Scene view shadows 1–32, scene and post 33–45, overlays 46; Game view shadows 47–78, scene and post 79–91, game GUIs 92 (76 views each, see Game GUI), GUI Builder preview 168, ImGui 255; player shadows 0–31, scene and post 32–44, GUIs 45.

Scene file format (.scene: JSON with 2-space indent, entities depth-first in hierarchy order, components and fields in registration order, floats in shortest round-trip form, so re-saving an unchanged scene gives identical bytes):

{
  "version": 5,
  "entities": [
    { "id": 1, "name": "Directional Light", "parent": null,
      "components": {
        "Transform": { "position": [0, 4, 0], "rotation": [-0.408218, -0.23457, -0.109382, 0.875426], "scale": [1, 1, 1] },
        "DirectionalLight": { "color": [1, 0.95, 0.9], "intensity": 1.1 } } },
    { "id": 4, "name": "Sphere", "parent": null,
      "components": {
        "Transform": { "position": [1.5, 0.75, 0], "rotation": [0, 0, 0, 1], "scale": [1.5, 1.5, 1.5] },
        "MeshRenderer": { "primitive": "sphere", "material": "materials/blue.mat" },
        "Rotator": { "axis": [0, 1, 0], "degreesPerSecond": 45 } } },
    { "id": 5, "name": "Satellite", "parent": 4,
      "components": {
        "Transform": { "position": [0.9, 0.6, 0], "rotation": [0, 0, 0.258819, 0.965926], "scale": [0.3, 0.3, 0.3] },
        "MeshRenderer": { "primitive": "cube", "material": "" } } },
    { "id": 6, "name": "Main Camera", "parent": null,
      "components": {
        "Transform": { "position": [0, 2.5, 6], "rotation": [-0.173648, 0, 0, 0.984808], "scale": [1, 1, 1] },
        "Camera": { "verticalFovDegrees": 60, "nearPlane": 0.1, "farPlane": 1000 } } }
  ],
  "systems": [],
  "lighting": { "ambientColor": [1, 1, 1], "ambientIntensity": 0.2 },
  "editor": { "camera": { "pivot": [0, 0.75, 0], "yaw": 20, "pitch": -20, "distance": 8 } }
}
  • id: persistent nonzero integer, unique in the file. parent is the parent's id, or null for a root. Children keep file order.
  • components: keyed by component type name; built-ins first (registration order), then Lua components (sorted by name), then preserved components of unknown type (sorted by name). For a built-in component every field is required, except the shadow fields below: added after scenes were saved without them, a missing one takes its default (no scene version change).
    • Transform (required): local position, rotation (quaternion), scale, relative to the parent.
    • MeshRenderer: primitive is cube, plane or sphere; material is a project-relative .mat file, empty for the default grey material. Scenes from before materials, with a color field instead, still load: color is dropped with a Console warning (the mesh renders with the default material). castShadows [true], receiveShadows [true] (see Shadows above).
    • DirectionalLight: color (linear RGB 0–1), intensity, castShadows [true], bias [0.05, >= 0], normalBias [1.5, >= 0], maxDistance [100, > 0], cascadeCount [4, 1–4].
    • PointLight: color (linear RGB 0–1), intensity (>= 0), range (meters, > 0), castShadows [false], bias [0.05, >= 0].
    • SpotLight: color (linear RGB 0–1), intensity (>= 0), range (meters, > 0), innerAngle and outerAngle (half-angles in degrees, 0 < innerAngle <= outerAngle < 90; a new one gets 30 and 45), castShadows [false], bias [0.05, >= 0].
    • AreaLight: color (linear RGB 0–1), intensity (>= 0; a new one gets 5), width and height (meters, > 0; a new one is 1 x 1), range (meters, > 0), twoSided [false], castShadows [false], bias [0.05, >= 0].
    • ReflectionProbe: boxSize (meters, > 0 on every axis; an axis-aligned box centered on the entity's world position, rotation and scale ignored), resolution (64, 128 or 256), intensity (>= 0), priority (integer, higher wins where boxes overlap) and bakedPath (project-relative .ktx written by the editor's Bake; empty = not baked). See Reflection probes.
    • Camera: camera looking down the entity's world -Z (scale ignored). projection is "perspective" (default) or "orthographic". Perspective uses verticalFovDegrees, which must be between 0 and 180 (exclusive). Orthographic uses orthographicSize (default 5, must be > 0): half the view's height in meters, its width that times the aspect ratio; the FOV is kept but unused. Both need 0 < nearPlane < farPlane. projection and orthographicSize are optional: scenes and prefabs saved without them load as perspective, size 5. The Inspector keeps the last valid value if an edit would break these rules. SSAO, SSR, depth of field, volumetric fog and shadows all work with either projection. The sky shows as one color through an orthographic camera (its rays are parallel).
    • Rotator: spins the entity in Play mode, degreesPerSecond around its local axis (normalized; zero = no spin).
    • StateMachine: gives the entity its own state machine in Play mode. machine is a definition name, initialState the state it enters at Play start (empty = the definition's initial). Both are plain strings: a name with no valid definition still loads and saves unchanged.
  • systems: Lua system scripts in run order, as paths relative to the project root (see Projects and Lua systems). Always written; optional when loading (missing = none).
  • lighting: the scene's ambient light, ambientColor (linear RGB 0–1) times ambientIntensity (>= 0). Always written; optional when loading (missing = white at 0.2), but if present both fields are required.
  • postProcessing: the scene's post-processing and fog, blocks tonemapping, bloom, colorGrading, vignette, fxaa, fog, each with enabled. Always written; optional when loading, and so is every block and field in it (missing = default, all effects off). Unknown blocks or fields, wrong types, out-of-range values and an operator other than None / ACES fail the load.
  • editor belongs to the editor (camera pivot, yaw/pitch in degrees, distance in meters); the engine keeps it as-is.
  • Loading rejects invalid JSON, a version other than 5 (versions 1–4 are not migrated: a version 4 file, the last one before prefabs, fails with unsupported "version" 4), a systems that isn't an array of non-empty strings, a missing Transform, unknown, missing or mistyped fields of a built-in component, unknown enum names, out-of-range Camera, PointLight, SpotLight, AreaLight or ReflectionProbe values, a malformed lighting or postProcessing, duplicate ids, parents that don't exist, and parent cycles. It reports why and leaves the current scene untouched. Lua components are lenient (schema drift), and a component name that is neither built-in nor a valid Lua definition is kept verbatim with a Console warning (missing definitions); see Lua components.

Prefab instances and .prefab files

A .prefab (see Prefabs) is a project file holding one entity subtree, in the scene format with only version (5) and entities: exactly one root, which may not itself be a prefab instance. Its entity ids are prefab-local: they stay the same across edits of the prefab, so instances keep pointing at the right entity. A prefab may hold instances of other prefabs (nesting), stored the same way as in a scene. Engine code: engine/prefab.h (instances, overrides, revert / apply / unpack) and engine/scene_io.h (loadPrefab, loadPrefabScene, savePrefab).

In a scene (or prefab) an instance is one entity record with prefab and instance instead of components:

{ "id": 10, "name": "Crate", "parent": null, "prefab": "prefabs/Crate.prefab",
  "instance": [
    { "source": 1, "id": 10,
      "components": { "Transform": { "position": [0, 0.5, 0], "rotation": [0, 0, 0, 1], "scale": [1, 1, 1] },
                      "MeshRenderer": { "material": "materials/gold_r05.mat" } } },
    { "source": 2, "id": 11, "name": "Lantern",
      "components": { "PointLight": { "color": [0.2, 0.6, 1] } },
      "added": { "Rotator": { "axis": [0, 1, 0], "degreesPerSecond": 45 } }, "removed": ["Collider"] },
    { "source": 3, "deleted": true }
  ] }
  • id, name, parent: the instance root's, as for any entity. The root's name and its whole Transform belong to the instance (they never follow the prefab and are never applied to it).
  • prefab: the project-relative .prefab.
  • instance: one entry per prefab entity, in the prefab's order. source is its prefab-local id, id its own PersistentId in this file (the entry whose id is the record's is the root's). Then only what differs from the prefab: name; components, the changed fields of components the prefab has (fields not listed follow the prefab); added, whole components the prefab doesn't have; removed, component names the instance doesn't have; deleted: true for a prefab child deleted from the instance (its subtree goes with it).
  • Children added to an instance are ordinary entity records after it whose parent is an instance entity's id.
  • An entity the prefab gained since the scene was saved gets a fresh id on load (past every id in the file) and is saved with it from then on; overrides of entities the prefab no longer has are dropped. An override that no longer validates against the prefab's other fields (e.g. a Collider radius past the prefab's new height) is dropped with a warning instead of failing the load. Lua components get their drift rules, unknown ones are kept verbatim.
  • A prefab that doesn't load (missing, unreadable, invalid, or one that would contain itself) doesn't fail the load: the instance becomes a placeholder entity named after the prefab file, at the root entry's Transform, with a SceneLoadReport warning naming the entity and path. Saving writes its record back unchanged (only id and parent follow edits), so nothing is lost; children added under its entities stay attached to the placeholder and keep their saved parent.
  • Saving is deterministic: entries in prefab order, keys in the order above, components in save order, so a load → save → save of a scene or prefab with instances gives identical bytes.
