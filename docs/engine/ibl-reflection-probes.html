Image-based lighting and reflection probes

A mesh's ambient light (everything that isn't a directional, point, spot or area light) can come from an environment: the scene's sky, or a reflection probe the editor baked from the scene around it. Diffuse ambient is the environment's irradiance (soft light from every side: blue from above under a blue sky), specular ambient is the environment reflected, sharp on smooth surfaces and blurred on rough ones. The Scene view, the Game view and the built game all render it the same way (engine/ibl.h, engine/shaders/fs_mesh.sc).

Sky lighting

  • Sky off: the flat ambient as before IBL: ambientColor * ambientIntensity for diffuse and a flat, Fresnel-weighted specular term. Scenes without a sky (and without baked probes) render pixel for pixel as before.
  • Sky on (procedural or cubemap): the sky replaces the flat ambient. ambientIntensity scales it (0 removes it) and ambientColor tints it: the default 0.2 lights with a fifth of the sky's own brightness; 1 lights with the sky as it is drawn. A cubemap that falls back to the procedural gradient (a bad face, a corrupt cube file) lights with the gradient too.
  • The environment is rebuilt only when the sky changes (mode, colors, faces or cube file, also from Lua with kast.sky.set): the Lighting panel's edits light the scene in the same frame. A scene or project switch frees it.

How it is built: the sky is drawn into a 128 x 128 cube (RGBA16F) on the GPU, which is box-filtered into a mip chain and GGX-prefiltered (split-sum, Karis 2013; filtered importance sampling, 128 samples per texel) into a cube whose level i is roughness i / 5 (levels 128 down to 4 x 4 are roughness 0 .. 1; the smaller ones repeat roughness 1). All of it runs as compute passes (engine/shaders/cs_ibl.sc) at the start of the frame's scene view; a GPU without compute shaders keeps the flat ambient and warns once. The diffuse part is order-2 spherical harmonics (9 RGB coefficients) computed on the CPU from the sky's colors (procedural) or its face images / cube file (cubemap), passed as shader constants: no texture stage.

Shading (both the built-in material and every .kshader / shader graph, in the ambient term after the lights):

diffuse  = (1 - F) * (1 - metallic) * albedo * SH(n)           // irradiance / pi
specular = prefiltered(R, roughness) * (F0 * brdf.x + brdf.y)  // split sum
ambient  = (diffuse + specular) * AO

F is the roughness-aware Schlick Fresnel the flat ambient uses, R the reflection of the view about the normal, and brdf Karis' analytic fit of the split-sum's BRDF integral ("Physically Based Shading on Mobile", clamped to 0..1): no lookup texture. The sky's environment is tinted by the ambient color and intensity; a probe's by its intensity.

Reflection probes

A ReflectionProbe component (GameObject > Reflection Probe) marks an axis-aligned box. Meshes inside it take their ambient from the probe's baked cube instead of the sky: a chrome ball in a red-and-green room reflects the walls, not the sky.

Which probe a mesh uses

Exactly one environment per mesh draw, chosen by the center of its world bounds: of the probes whose box contains that point (edges included), the highest priority; among equal priorities the probe whose box center is nearest; then the first in the scene. A mesh in no box uses the sky's environment (or the flat ambient with the sky off). Probes don't blend: a mesh crossing a box edge switches as its center crosses it.

Box projection

A plain cube reflection looks as if the room were infinitely far away. Probes are box-projected (parallax corrected): the reflection ray from the shaded point is followed to where it leaves the probe's box, and the cube is sampled in the direction from the probe's center (where it was baked) to that point. A sphere near the left wall therefore sees that wall large and close. Fit the box to the room's walls for the best result. From outside the box (a pixel of a large mesh whose center is inside) the plain direction is used.

Bake

In the editor only (edit mode, a scene saved in a project): the probe's Inspector section has Bake, and Window > Lighting has Bake All Probes (--bake-probes on the command line). For each probe the Scene view's renderer draws the six cube faces at the probe's position at its resolution, with the scene's current lighting, meshes, models and terrain and its sky; particles, gizmos and editor overlays are left out, and probes don't light the bake (one bounce: the meshes in it are lit by the sky). The faces are prefiltered as the sky is (envMips: 64 → 5 levels, 128 → 6, 256 → 7, then roughness 1 down to 1 x 1), read back, and written next to the scene:

<scene-dir>/<scene-name>_probes/<entity id>.ktx    the prefiltered cube: RGBA16F, full mip chain
<scene-dir>/<scene-name>_probes/<entity id>.json   its spherical harmonics

The entity id is the scene's persistent entity id ("id" in the scene file), so a probe keeps its files across saves and renames. Bake sets bakedPath and marks the scene dirty (not an undo step); re-baking overwrites the files and every view reloads them. The bake takes a few frames, during which the window doesn't redraw.

The SH sidecar (JSON, not KTX metadata: readable, and written atomically beside the cube):

{ "version": 1, "size": 64, "sh": [[0.49, 0.48, 0.56], [0.18, 0.10, -0.10], ...] }

sh holds the 9 RGB coefficients of the radiance convolved with the clamped cosine and divided by pi, in Kast's world axes: irradiance / pi at unit normal n = c0 + c1 y + c2 z + c3 x + c4 xy + c5 yz + c6 (3z² - 1) + c7 xz + c8 (x² - y²). Cube sides are in bgfx's order (+X, -X, +Y, -Y, +Z, -Z) with Kast's +Z on the cube's -Z side, as for sky cube files.

Build ships the .ktx and .json in game.pak as they are; the player loads them like the editor.

When a probe can't be used

Not baked, a missing or unreadable .ktx or .json, a corrupt file, or a bake of another resolution than the probe's (baked at 64, but its resolution is 128 (bake it again)): the engine warns once, naming the probe (Reflection probe 'Room Probe': 'room_probes/10.ktx': cannot read the file; meshes in its box are lit as outside it), and its meshes use the sky's environment (or the flat ambient) exactly as if the probe weren't there. The scene still loads, nothing is baked automatically, and the probe's Inspector shows the reason in red (or Not baked / Baked).

Texture stages

The mesh shader already used all 16 Direct3D 11 sampler stages. The environment cube (s_env) takes stage 8, which held the occlusion texture: occlusion now shares the metallic-roughness texture (stage 1) in glTF's ORM layout, R = occlusion, G = roughness, B = metallic (this choice was approved by the user over dropping a shadow cube or a .kshader texture param). A material whose occlusion and metallic-roughness are the same image (an ORM texture) samples it as is; one with only one of them samples that one (the other's channels read 1); one with two different images gets them packed into one on the CPU when it loads (occlusion's R resampled to the metallic-roughness image's size if they differ), cooked like any texture, and Build ships the packed one. occlusionStrength works as before, and .kshader's sampleOcclusion(uv).r and sampleMetallicRoughness(uv) still return the occlusion and the metallic-roughness (G, B). Materials with at most one of those images render pixel for pixel as before.

Lua

ReflectionProbe is a regular component: kast.get(e, "ReflectionProbe") and kast.set(e, "ReflectionProbe", {intensity = 0.5, priority = 2}) (validated as the scene file is). There is no Lua bake: probes are baked in the editor. The sky's lighting follows kast.sky.set.

Limits

  • Baked only: probes don't update while the game runs (no realtime capture, no screen-space reflections).
  • One probe per mesh, no blending between probes, no probe arrays; diffuse is per probe, not a light-probe grid.
  • The bake is one bounce: probes don't see each other's reflections.
  • Bake has no undo; re-bake or clear bakedPath instead.
